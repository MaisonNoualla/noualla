Our Story

Restoration came first.

Noualla began in 2022 on a 9,300 m² hillside near Oued Laou in northern Morocco.

Before expanding accommodation, the founding team spent four years planting trees, improving water retention, establishing food gardens and learning how to work with the landscape.

Today, Noualla is moving from a privately funded land-restoration project and small guest pilot into a carefully scaled regenerative rural retreat.

Tourism is not the destination. It is the engine that allows restoration to continue.

Guest Validation

A guest experience already tested

Since 2023, Noualla's existing 45 m² house has welcomed more than 100 overnight guest groups through private word of mouth.

Approximately one-third returned, while many additional guests arrived through personal referrals — without public advertising or an online booking platform.

Guests consistently value the peace, landscape, house design, fresh fruit and the experience of picking herbs directly from the garden. Their most frequent request is for food service and more structured activities on the property.

A Future Rooted in Simplicity

The next phase

The next phase will add four small bungalows, a farm-to-table restaurant and a flexible community workshop space. The accommodation will be carefully dispersed across the hillside rather than concentrated in one building. The intended material palette includes local stone, earth-based construction, wood and reeds.

Future experiences will include —

Local cooking experiences

Discover Moroccan cuisine through intimate meals prepared with local ingredients and traditional knowledge.

Local pottery workshops

Learn from local artisans and experience the craft traditions that connect generations.

Farm walks

Explore the gardens, terraces and ecosystems that make the land come alive.

Seasonal harvest experiences

Participate in planting, harvesting and the rhythms of agricultural life.

Nature & night experiences

Stargazing, quiet walks and discovering the hidden life of the Mediterranean hills.

Community

Local people are partners, not attractions

Approximately two kilometres from Noualla, women in Frane Ali continue a pottery tradition passed from mothers and grandmothers to their daughters.

Two pottery families have verbally agreed to participate in future Noualla experiences. Additional local cooks, guides, horse owners, farmers and food providers have also been identified.

Community providers will determine their own availability, group sizes, pricing and what elements of their traditions they choose to share.

A plan taking shape. Slowly.

Noualla is intentionally being built step by step. Every tree planted, every terrace restored and every detail created brings the vision closer to life.
