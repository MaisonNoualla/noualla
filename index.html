Before Hospitality, There Was Regeneration

From degraded hillside to living landscape.

In 2022, a severely degraded hillside near a rural village in northern Morocco was acquired with one purpose: to prove that damaged land could come back to life. The land had suffered years of overgrazing, erosion, and declining biodiversity.

The first goal was not to build accommodation. It was to restore a relationship with the land. Through patience, observation, permaculture principles, and continuous care, the hillside has gradually transformed into a diverse living ecosystem.

Today, one permanent worker maintains the land and its systems day to day, with additional help brought in during planting seasons and larger projects.

Why Noualla Exists

Hospitality grew from the land.

Noualla was not created to build another hotel. The hospitality concept emerged from watching the land regenerate, and wanting to share that experience with others — a place where people can slow down, reconnect with nature, discover local traditions, and experience a different way of travelling.

The Living Farm

Walk through a regenerating landscape and discover fruit trees, medicinal plants, native species, insects and pollinators, and ecological farming practices in daily use.

Nature & Exploration

Discover the surrounding region through mountain walks, river landscapes, nearby waterfalls, the Mediterranean coastline, and rural villages.

Traditional Craftsmanship

Meet local artisans and discover generations of knowledge. Guests will have the opportunity to visit traditional pottery makers, learn from local women artisans, create their own pieces, and support handmade craftsmanship. More on this partnership below, under Community.

Slow Moments

Sometimes the most meaningful experiences are simple: sitting on a private terrace, listening to the wind, watching birds and animals, observing village life from a distance, and enjoying the changing light over the mountains.

A Small-Scale Model

Small by choice, never a resort.

Noualla is intentionally limited to protect the character of the land and preserve the quality of the experience. Fewer guests means more privacy, deeper experiences, lighter environmental impact, and stronger relationships with the surrounding community.

4 bungalows

The Name Noualla

A name rooted in Moroccan heritage.

Noualla takes its name from the traditional rural Moroccan dwelling built from natural materials — reeds, earth, wood — simple in form, but rich in meaning.

For generations, these homes sheltered families, stories, and everyday life. Built from what the land provided, they represented a way of living connected to nature, climate, and community.

Noualla carries that philosophy forward: simplicity, connection, and respect for the land.

Founder Story

A personal journey that became a shared vision.

Noualla began as a personal commitment to restore a piece of damaged land. The founder's journey was shaped by years of travelling through developing countries, learning from rural communities, indigenous cultures, and regenerative projects around the world.

Experiences in places such as Panama, Belize, Zanzibar, and Morocco revealed both the beauty of community-based living and the challenges created by unsustainable tourism. Noualla brings those lessons together: a place where visitors are welcomed not only as guests, but as participants in a more conscious way of travelling.
